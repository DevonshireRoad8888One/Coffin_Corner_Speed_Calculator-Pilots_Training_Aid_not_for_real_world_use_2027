Storm avoidance - why 8 miles is not a safe blanket rule


STANDARDS: 10 NM from ANY thunderstorm | 20 NM from SEVERE | 20 NM IFR avoidance.
Measure from the MOST INTENSE PART of the cell, never from the nearest visible echo.












WHY 8 NM FAILS - FOUR REASONS

1 REACTION TIME - 8 NM is only 64 seconds at 450 kt. You cannot clear a cell, get a clearance and descend in 64 seconds. 20 NM is 160.

2 THE HAZARD EXTENDS BEYOND THE RAIN - microburst outflow and damaging wind reach 10-20 NM from the core; hail travels far downwind.

3 REFLECTIVITY IS NOT THE MEASURE - in THIS accident the aircraft flew through 25-35 dBZ at FL350 while the violent core was 10,000 ft BELOW it. A pilot looking at radar at the right flight level could have seen something that did not look like a major storm. The upper flank of a cell, where the updraft, shear and icing live, can look MILD where you are and be lethal.

4 CELLS MOVE AND GROW - the buffer erodes while you are using it.







What should have been recognised BEFORE the upset


    

  • In cloud with ice, your stall speed is rising and nothing shows it. At constant Mach, IAS falls about 5.7 kt per 1,000 ft in a climb - if it falls FASTER, ice is eating your margin. That trend is the only instrument that sees it.
    • 

  • Same thrust, less climb than the book = ice. SEP falling with no speed change = ice.
    • 

  • The computer's going to be wrong. FMS and autothrust climb performance assumes a CLEAN wing. In icing, actual performance is worse than displayed, and the cleared level may be physically unreachable. Override the automation when the aircraft behaves differently from the machine model.
    • 

  • Climbing to get above weather is backwards when the freezing level is below you. The safe direction is DOWN and OUT.
    • 

  • "Overspeed" is a SYMPTOM of the dive, not the cause. The likely reaction - pull to slow down - tightens the spiral in a bank and is fatal.
    • 

  • Stall recognition window in a jet: 7 to 10 seconds. With ice the stall can arrive with NO buffet, at a LOWER critical AoA than trained, and asymmetric. The cure is PUSH FORWARD - counterintuitive, and the instinct (pull) is what makes it fatal. AF447 (2009): the same human dynamic, a different mechanism (no ice; a frozen AOA sensor). Borrow the lesson, not the cause.
    • 

  • Severe turbulence severity is judged by aircraft performance, not comfort. "Unable to hold altitude" = SEVERE. Report it that way, and treat it as a maintenance event.
    •

ISA reference atmosphere - sound speed in KNOTS


Unit check. At 35,000 ft, a = 500.5 kt = 576.2 mph. The mph figure is easily mistaken for a knot figure.
Tropopause: the ISA is isothermal above 36,089 ft (11,000 m), so a = 497.9 kt at every level from FL370 to FL450 - it stops falling with altitude. Above FL360 the stall Mach rises from DENSITY ALONE.

Equations and assumptions


ATMOSPHERE - ICAO US Standard Atmosphere 1976-80
  h_m = alt_ft*0.3048 ;  T_ISA = 288.15 - 0.0065*h_m  [K]
  TROPOPAUSE at h_m = 11,000 m: T = 216.65 K, CONSTANT above.
  p = 101325*(1 - 0.0065*h_m/288.15)^5.25588  [Pa]
      above 11,000 m: p = 22632.1*exp(-g*(h-11000)/(R*216.65))
  T = OAT+273.15 (or T_ISA) ; rho = p/(R*T), R = 287.05287 ; sigma = rho/1.225
  a = sqrt(1.4*R*T) = m/s -> x1.68781 = kt -> x1.15078 = mph
  a*sqrt(sigma) = a_kt*sqrt(sigma)   [kt]  (IAS per unit Mach)

STALL
  Vs(W)   = Vs_ref*sqrt(W/W_ref) + config_inc   [KIAS]
  Vs_ice  = Vs(W)*(1 + ice%/100)
  Mstall  = Vs_ice/(a*sqrt(sigma))

CEILING (more restrictive limit always binds)
  MMO_eff = stepped Mach if the step applies, else general MMO
  M_VMO   = VMO(KIAS)/(a*sqrt(sigma))
  Ceiling = min(MMO_eff, M_VMO)

POLICY WINDOW
  Floor = (Vs_ice + stall_margin)/(a*sqrt(sigma))
  Ceiling_pol = Ceiling - overspeed_margin/(a*sqrt(sigma))
  If Floor >= Ceiling_pol -> BAND CLOSED -> NO-GO at any Mach

ICE COUNTDOWN
  margin_kt = IAS_now - Vs_ice ; zero when ice% = (IAS_now/Vs_W - 1)*100
  policy window closes when ice% = ((Ceiling_ias - overspeed_margin)/Vs_W - 1)*100

q
  V = Mach*a ; q = 0.5*rho*V^2 (1 psf = 47.8803 Pa)
  q_ratio = (rho/rho_ref)*(V/V_ref)^2  ref: 35,000 ft M0.76 ISA, q = 201 psf

IDENTITY: a*sqrt(sigma) = sqrt(gamma*p/rho0). The IAS at a given Mach and
  PRESSURE ALTITUDE is independent of temperature. OAT changes Mach, TAS and q.

ASSUMPTIONS
  - Weight scaling of stall speed is first-order (Vs with sqrt(W)).
  - Pressure taken at the 29.92-referenced PRESSURE ALTITUDE; OAT assumed valid there.
  - The estimated geometric altitude is a first-order approximation.
  - q is a structural-load proxy, not a certified limit.
  - Steady flight only. No gusts, manoeuvre loads, flutter or upset dynamics.
